Booth · Record anywhere

Your recordings stay on this device. You decide what to share.

1. Bring in a song

Open Local songs & export. Import a Booth session ZIP from your producer, or an audio beat from Files. For a raw beat, enter its BPM first. Wait for “Saved on this device” and the offline-ready message before going offline.

2. Record

Use the iPhone’s built-in microphone, a wired headset or a Bluetooth headset mic that Safari makes available. No iRig is required. Headphones help prevent the beat leaking into your vocal. Connect Bluetooth before enabling the mic. Open Input / Count-in, enable the microphone and allow access. The input menu offers System default and any microphones reported by the browser. Check the displayed recording input and make a short test take. Bluetooth routing and latency depend on iOS and the headset; keep Input monitor Off if monitoring is delayed. Choose your track and range, then Record. Keep Booth visible and your device unlocked. Stop, listen, and use your preferred take on the track.

3. Layer and punch

Add Ad-libs, Double, Harmony or Vocal to make a new track. Overdub plays existing layers while you record. To fix a line, arm its track, select Punch in/out, set In and Out and a pre-roll. Only the captured interval replaces the old phrase. Undo last edit restores it; raw takes remain available.

4. Listen and shape your vocal

The Vocal strip controls the armed track. Track and Master are your listening mix. Vocal FX are editable settings; recordings remain dry. Input monitor Off avoids delayed/doubled monitoring. Audition with the beat or vocal-only before choosing a take.

5. Choose what to send

Open Local songs & export → Export vocals & notes. Check only the tracks or takes you want to share. Add a note.

  • Mix handoff: dry sources, edits, timing and editable effects.
  • Aligned dry WAVs: edited tracks with silence from song start, for manual placement at zero.
  • Raw WAVs: original takes with timing notes.
  • Dry listening mix: beat and selected vocals, levels/pan included, no vocal FX, mono WAV.

Prepare the file, then Share via Messages/Mail, or Save to Files and attach it yourself. Sharing does not automatically import anything into Mix.

6. Back up

Save complete local backup includes the backing, all takes, current vocal edits/settings and lyrics. Keep it in Files. Restore on a new device by importing it. Reimporting an existing song opens the local copy without overwriting its takes. Browser data can be removed by the OS or by clearing website data; do not rely on it as your only copy.

Producer handoff

In Mix → Booth, export a session ZIP. Send it to the artist. Import their returned vocal package in the matching project. It adds new tracks and retains editable effects; identical packages do not create duplicates. Raw-beat sessions require explicit manual matching. If the arrangement changed, use raw WAVs and check timing.

Use the same Booth address and browser each time. Home Wi-Fi is not required for imported local songs. Microphone permissions, adapter routing and monitoring latency should be tested before an important session. Maximum song length: 20 minutes; each take: 10 minutes; ZIP package: 256 MB.

More timeline space · remove a clip

Tap Mixer, Vocal FX or Lyrics in the bottom bar to collapse or reopen that section. Hide panels gives the timeline the available height. Your layout stays on this device.

Hold a vocal clip for a moment, then drag it up into the bin and release. Release outside the bin to cancel. Undo restores the clip; the original recording stays in your takes. The backing track stays locked. Clip removal is unavailable while recording or publishing.

Focused recording workflow

Use Timeline, Mixer, Vocal FX and Lyrics in the bottom navigation. Your tracks and takes stay in the session when changing screens. Arm / Record opens setup: choose the vocal track, range and recording mode, enable your mic, then adjust backing, vocal and headphone levels. Start recording closes setup and returns to the growing waveform. Pause recording freezes the beat and capture; Resume continues the same take. Stop saves the captured audio, including when paused. Takes autosave locally. Save session backup creates a file for safekeeping; Sessions opens your library without deleting the current song.
